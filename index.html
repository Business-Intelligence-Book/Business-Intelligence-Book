Business Intelligence and Analytics

Data-Driven Decision Making, and Fundamental AI Concepts

How does a streaming service seem to know what you will watch next? Why does a bank refuse one loan and approve another? What is a fair price for that used car? With the right data and a basic understanding of AI, questions like these become answerable, and decisions become better informed.

Business Intelligence and Analytics is a clear, practical introduction to the methods behind modern analytics, data, and AI. It covers data cleaning, exploratory analysis, visual storytelling, clustering, association rules, regression, classification and model evaluation, as well as the social questions raised by generative AI.

Each idea is explained with small examples and connected to real cases from business and history. Fun facts and short biographies show where the ideas came from and why they still matter.

The book is written for students, professionals and anyone who wants to understand the basic concepts of AI and data science. No programming background is required, only curiosity.

Presentation slides and Python code with exercises are available for every chapter, for instructors who would like to use this book in their classroom.

Business Intelligence and Analytics

Exercises and Solutions

This is the companion workbook to the textbook Business Intelligence and Analytics. It turns the ideas of business intelligence and analytics into practice, from cleaning a messy table to choosing a model and checking whether a chart tells the truth.

Each chapter opens with short questions that test your understanding, followed by problems set in everyday situations: online shops, banks, streaming services, hospitals and grocery stores. The chapters follow the textbook, from data cleaning, exploratory analysis and visualization to clustering, association rules, regression, classification and the social implications of AI. Most problems need only pen, paper and a calculator.

The book is written for students, professionals and anyone who wants to practice turning data into decisions, in a classroom or on their own.

About the author

Michalis Vlachos is a Professor at the Faculty of Business and Economics (HEC Lausanne), University of Lausanne, and a former researcher at IBM Research in Zurich and New York. He works on data science, recommender systems and information retrieval, with a focus on interpretable, scalable methods for real-world decision support.
